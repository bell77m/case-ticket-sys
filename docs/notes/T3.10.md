# T3.10 NGINX Ingress

Date: 2026-09-28 · Requirements: NFR-1, NFR-4, FR-A11, NFR-3, FR-P3, FR-T1 · Agent: ops (main agent); Go part by another session (2026-09-27)

## What was done
- The F5 NGINX Ingress Controller (Helm chart 2.7.3, controller 5.6.3) fronts the app. Its values are in deploy/platform:
  - snippets are off;
  - `externalTrafficPolicy: Local` keeps client addresses;
  - server tokens are off.
- `deploy/base/ingress.yaml` is a mergeable Ingress:
  - the master holds the host, the TLS certificate and the `company-network` Policy, which applies to every path (NFR-4);
  - minion `/`: bodies up to 101 MB;
  - minion `/api/events`: no buffering and a 1 h read timeout;
  - minion `/api/track`: 30 requests/min per client address, burst 20, 429 past it, no queueing.
  Plain HTTP redirects to HTTPS.
- The app believes `X-Forwarded-For` only from the pod network (`TRUSTED_PROXIES=10.42.0.0/16`). NGINX appends the real peer (`$proxy_add_x_forwarded_for`), so login limits, guest limits and audit IPs see each client.
- A NetworkPolicy lets only the ingress controller's namespace and Gotenberg reach the app pods, so no other pod can forge client IPs or skip the allow list.
- `make cluster-check-ingress` (deploy/local/ingress-check.sh) proves all of this on the local k3s cluster.

## Decisions
- Proven on the local k3s cluster (user's choice, 2026-09-28), not the real host. Still to do on T3.09:
  - the company CA certificate as `ticket-app-tls` (from Vault via VSO, T3.11);
  - the company networks in Policy `company-network`. The base placeholder is TEST-NET-1, which allows nobody: it fails closed until set;
  - the real host name in place of `tickets-staging.example.internal` and `tickets.example.internal`.
- Mergeable Ingress (master + minions) is the F5 controller's way to give paths their own settings on one host; plain duplicate Ingresses for one host are not allowed.
- `/api/track`: 30 r/min, burst 20, `nodelay`. Without `nodelay` NGINX only delays a client that sends one request at a time, and never rejects it.
- `TRUSTED_PROXIES` stays the pod CIDR, and the NetworkPolicy makes the ingress the only pod-network caller that matters. The security review rated the CIDR alone as high. Pod IPs change, so the policy, not a narrower CIDR, is the fix.
- Gotenberg may reach the app (it prints `/print/*`); it could send a forged header, but it only loads our own print pages.
- Locally the "company network" is 172.16.0.0/12 (Docker's address pool). In-cluster pods (10.42.x) are outside it, which is how "an outside IP is refused" is tested.
